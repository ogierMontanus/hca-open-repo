Andersen's Hvem Hvad Hvor

About the project, source material and method

Andersen's Hvem Hvad Hvor ("Andersen's Who What Where") registers proper names in H.C. Andersen's works. Its foundation is the people, artworks and places Andersen himself mentions:

  • the people he meets (who — hvem)
  • the artworks he mentions (what — hvad)
  • the places he describes (where — hvor)

Version 1

Version 1 of Andersen's Hvem Hvad Hvor registers mentions in H.C. Andersen's diaries. The project builds on the ten diary volumes published in print between 1971–1977, together with the two accompanying index volumes:

  • Volume XI, Personregister (Index of Persons), 1977
  • Volume XII, Værk-, sag- og stedregister (Index of Works, Subjects and Places), 1976

The index volumes are central, because they gather and systematize the named-entity material from the ten diary volumes. Below, the project's setting, source material and purpose are described under the headings where, what and why. The description is supplemented with information on how the material has been digitized.

As noted, Version 1 builds on the diaries. Similar indexes exist for, among other things, Andersen's correspondence and his printed works, published in 18 volumes by the Danish Society for Language and Literature (Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab). These works, too, are packed with information about people, personal names, place names and artworks.

At present, researchers interested in a given place or a particular artist must therefore turn to quite a number of different sources to build up their knowledge. Digitization can hopefully connect these data silos in the longer term, making it possible to search across the various indexes and groups of works.

1. Where?

The work is carried out at the Hans Christian Andersen Centre, University of Southern Denmark, Odense.

Holger Berg is principally responsible for the digitization. Assisting him are Søren Dall-Hansen and Ole Lebech. The two citizen scientists work as volunteers and have backgrounds as, respectively, a surveyor and an IT consultant.

The project is thus anchored in a research environment, while also drawing on skills in digitization and broad accessibility. This combination is necessary to turn the printed indexes and diaries into material that can be searched and cross-linked.

2. What?

Andersen's surviving diaries are held at the Royal Danish Library (Det Kongelige Bibliotek). The project's primary basis is the twelve volumes published by the Danish Society for Language and Literature, G.E.C. Gads Forlag.

The printed edition

H.C. Andersens dagbøger I–X (H.C. Andersen's Diaries I–X), together with the two index volumes XI and XII, were published between 1971–1977 by the Danish Society for Language and Literature at G.E.C. Gads Forlag. A photographic reprint was published in paperback in 1995–96.

The transcription of the original diary manuscripts, held at the Royal Danish Library, and the preparation of the notes etc., were carried out by Tue Gad and Kirsten Weber of the Royal Danish Library's Department of Manuscripts, together with Helga Vang Lauridsen, who was likewise employed there. The two index volumes were compiled by Helge Topsøe-Jensen.

The Royal Danish Library's digital edition

The first digital edition was published in 2004. This, too, was the result of a larger collaboration and was led by Bruno Svindborg of the Department of Manuscripts.

The current digital edition, from 2024, is a reissue of the technically outdated 2004 digital edition and contains the ten proper diary volumes as digital text together with facsimiles and illustrations. The project was led by Bo Krantz Simonsen. The two index volumes were included, untranscribed, in the 2004 edition, but were left out of the 2024 edition.

Rønlev's digitization

Around 2022, all twelve volumes were scanned for the Danish Society for Language and Literature by Claus Rønlev. Rønlev's digitizations are available as PDF files.

  • Volume XI – Personregister, 1977 – Rønlev↗ (opens in a new tab)
  • Volume XII – Værk-, sag- og stedregister, 1976 – Rønlev↗ (opens in a new tab)

The same volumes are also available as facsimiles on Tekstnet:

  • Volume XI – Personregister, 1977 – Tekstnet↗ (opens in a new tab)
  • Volume XII – Værk-, sag- og stedregister, 1976 – Tekstnet↗ (opens in a new tab)

Thorough OCR recognition was carried out, and Rønlev's material thus forms the basis for an improved digital edition of index volumes XI and XII. As part of the project, the OCR text has been reviewed and corrected.

For the ten diary volumes, we use the Royal Danish Library's two versions together with versions that Dan Andersen of Odense City Museums has prepared for his own use and made available.

The digitization thus builds on several layers of source material: the printed volumes, the Royal Danish Library's digital edition, Rønlev's scans and OCR processing, and the subsequent manual corrections. The purpose of gathering and processing this material is to create a more reliable and usable basis for registering the people, artworks and places in Andersen's diaries.

3. Why?

The index volumes were compiled by the distinguished H.C. Andersen scholar Henrik Topsøe Jensen. We do not reproduce everything from the index volumes and therefore do not include his articles, among them En registerarbejder siger farvel ("An Index-Maker Says Farewell"). Also omitted are the preface, the subject index and the family trees.

In Volume XII, Topsøe-Jensen regretted that the financial costs and his advanced age prevented the preparation of definitive annotations for the ten-volume edition. The index was therefore meant to serve as a kind of running commentary on the named-entity material. Our presentation of the diaries brings that material together with each page.

In the printed edition, there are considerable costs attached to every lookup. For that reason, Jensen italicized the page numbers he judged most significant. In the digital edition, the italics have been omitted, because the information can be found directly and connected with a single click. That is one of the main purposes of the digitization: to make the material easier to search and to shorten the distance between individual pieces of information.

The digitization also makes it possible to connect different types of named-entity material across the volumes. A user interested in an artist such as Michelangelo can, for example, cross-reference the artworks Andersen saw and mentioned in Volume XII with the places where Michelangelo is mentioned as a person in Volume XI.

In this way, Andersen's Hvem Hvad Hvor carries forward the original purpose of the indexes, while extending it with digital possibilities for searching, linking and comparison. Visualizations of places with known geocoordinates are one such targeted attempt. The digitized version consistently distinguishes between the index's original content and our enrichments, corrections and cross-links.

4. Who?

Our work builds on the many major efforts made over the years by Henrik Topsøe Jensen, the Danish Society for Language and Literature, the Royal Danish Library, and volunteers including Claus Rønlev, Ane Grum-Svendsen and Dan Andersen.

We thank everyone who has contributed to the foundation of this edition. We publish our work under the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) licence. This allows future researchers and users to build on the material, easing and enriching further research.

Version 1 of Andersen's Hvem Hvad Hvor has received generous support from the Svend Grundtvig and Axel Olrik Foundation (Svend Grundtvigs og Axel Olriks Legat).
